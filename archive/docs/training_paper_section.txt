Training and baseline methodology

We use Double DQN with a fully connected 2–64–64–4 network and ReLU hidden layers. The observations are normalized row and column coordinates, and the four outputs represent the cardinal moves. The online network selects the greedy next action for the Bellman target, and a separate target network evaluates that action. The target weights synchronize every 100 optimizer updates. Adam (learning rate 0.001) minimizes Huber loss with a gradient norm cap of 10. The discount factor is 0.99. Uniform minibatches of 64 are sampled from up to 10,000 transitions. Exploration decreases linearly from 1.0 toward 0.05 over the first 80% of the episode budget. Goal, collision, and timeout stop bootstrapping.

The unshielded policy executes the proposed action. The random-safe baseline samples uniformly from the safe set on intervention. Highest-Q replacement maximizes the online action value over that set. The fixed-penalty variant uses the same selector and subtracts the configured penalty from the training reward on intervention. The original adaptive mixture chooses random-safe with probability equal to the fraction of unsafe proposals in the preceding 100 actions, initialized at 0.5, and uses highest-Q otherwise. Replay records the executed action and resulting next state, while the training log reports the environment reward and tracks unsafe proposal and intervention counts separately. This penalty acts on the executed safe transition and provides no direct target for the rejected proposal.

The final comparison requires matched resolved training settings, recorded interaction and optimizer counts, and greedy shield-off evaluation on common scenarios. Preliminary standard DQN runs and corrected Double DQN smoke runs are implementation evidence. Comparative results will be reported from the finalized multi-method runs.
